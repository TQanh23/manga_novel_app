The sign above the door read EMBER'S BAKERY in careful gold letters, and underneath, in slightly smaller letters, NO KNIGHTS PLEASE. Ember had painted both lines herself, holding the brush in her teeth.

Opening day began badly. The first customer, a farmer named Bertram, took one look at the eight-metre dragon behind the counter, dropped his basket and ran all the way back to the village without stopping.

"That went well," said Pip, the kitchen boy, who had been hired mostly because he was the only applicant. Ember sighed, and a small puff of smoke curled out of her nostrils and set off the brand-new bell over the door.

The problem, she reflected, was reputation. Six hundred years of burning castles and hoarding gold had given dragons a certain image. Nobody ever remembered that she had also invented the perfect oven temperature, simply by breathing on things.

By noon, three more villagers had come close enough to peer through the window, and one brave child had pressed her nose to the glass. Ember slid a tray of cinnamon rolls into the display, very slowly, so as not to alarm anyone.

The child came in. She was perhaps seven, with a gap in her front teeth and the steely expression of someone who had been told not to do exactly this. "Are you going to eat me?" she asked.

"Certainly not," said Ember. "Children are far too stringy. Would you like a cinnamon roll instead?" The girl considered this, nodded, and took the roll. Then she took a bite. Then she ran out of the shop shouting for her entire family.
